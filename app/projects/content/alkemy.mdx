Democratize the rainmaker. alkemy turns the relationship discipline that grows expert firms into a system the whole firm can run.

## The Problem

Every advisory firm has one or two rainmakers, and everyone else. When you ask owners where revenue actually comes from, the answer is almost always the same: 70 to 90 percent from referrals and warm relationships. Growth lives in a handful of heads, and that creates three quiet problems:

- **Concentrated** — active business development runs through one or two people. When they slow down, retire, or leave, the firm's growth goes with them.
- **Unsystematic** — a good touch only happens when there is a real reason to reach out, and finding that reason (a trigger plus something credible to say) is manual research nobody has time for.
- **Decaying** — the longer since the last touch, the harder the re-entry. Contact lists rot in spreadsheets, CRMs are storage with no brain, and relationships silently go cold until a competitor shows up at the moment that mattered.

## Why Now

AI is commoditizing the analyst layer that expert firms used to bill for: the research, the first-draft deck, the market map, the memo. As that work gets cheap, the durable edge shifts to the one thing a competitor cannot download — the relationship, applied at the right moment. When analysis becomes cheap, relationship intelligence becomes one of the few advantages left.

## What a Rainmaker Actually Does

We usually explain the rainmaker with the word charisma, as if it were a gift. It is not. The edge is a discipline almost nobody else can afford to run by hand:

1. Hold a live list of the people who matter.
2. Notice when something changes in their world.
3. Always show up with something worth saying.

Run by hand, this costs 15 to 20 minutes of research per person per touch, so it caps at what one head can hold. The rest of the firm's pipeline stays hostage to referral luck.

## The Solution: A Keep-in-Touch Engine

alkemy turns that discipline into a system the whole firm runs. Every key account gets a brain. Every key person inside it gets watched. When something changes, whoever owns that relationship gets both the reason to reach out and the thing worth saying — grounded in real signals, with honest citations, on their live accounts rather than a canned demo.

This is the opposite of mass outreach. Broad, generic sending is cheap, easy to automate, and worth less every day. Well-timed relevance to the right person about something they actually care about is much harder to replicate, and it is exactly what alkemy makes repeatable.

## Three Outcomes

The result shows up three ways, and different buyers pay for different ones:

- **Growth — more at-bats.** Get in front of more of the right rooms, early enough to matter.
- **Succession — durability.** A firm that no longer depends on one or two people's books.
- **Leverage — origination.** Juniors who can originate, not just deliver.

## How It Works

1. **Key people inside key accounts** — a named-person list attached to each account, editable and owned.
2. **Last touch and cadence** — per person, when you last connected and the target rhythm for staying in touch.
3. **Something worth saying, per person** — signals resolved to the person level, not just the account, so the reason to reach out is specific.
4. **The weekly list** — one view of who to touch this week and why, ordered by decay and by how strong the trigger is.

## The Vision

There are tens of thousands of small expert firms, and most are squeezed from two sides at once: AI is eating the analyst work they used to bill for, and their growth engine — the rainmaker — does not scale and does not last.

alkemy exists so those firms can grow past their founders and outlive them. It takes what a firm's best rainmaker keeps in his head — who matters and when to show up — and turns it into institutional memory that can outlast any one person. The relationship discipline that builds these firms becomes learnable, runnable, and durable.

The result: tens of thousands of small firms stay independent and alive, instead of dying with their rainmaker or being absorbed because they never built a way to grow that did not depend on one or two people.
